## Positions found in his Q&A answers

These are his stated positions, not consensus claims:

- An AI engineer is first and foremost an engineer; without tests, monitoring, and engineering rigor, tool-clicking work is not engineering
- Evaluation is the most important skill and the best eval tool is the one you write yourself
- Traditional NLP is mostly legacy outside latency-critical and simple cases; fine-tuning is a niche specialization
- Coding agents are "enthusiastic interns" - useful but sloppy, so engineering processes (tests, CI/CD, review) remain essential
- Startups should hire generalists first and specialists when they reach ~50-60 people
- Learning in public and meetups beat cold outreach for job search
